UFO Lore, the "Races," and the Real Odds of Disclosure

1Bottom line up front

As of today, no government anywhere has officially confirmed non-human intelligence (NHI) or alien craft. The US is releasing more UFO material than ever, but what it has released is unresolved cases, not proof.

  • FactThe US has put out six tranches of "unresolved" case files under the new PURSUE program (May 8 – Sep 18, 2026). The Pentagon's UFO office, AARO, resolved 370 more cases in its FY2025 report, and every one turned out to be ordinary.
  • FactThe strongest disclosure mechanism on the table is a JFK-style UAP Records Review Board. The House's FY27 defense bill reportedly includes it. The Senate hasn't acted, and in 2023 the final bill dropped the same idea. The final bill is expected around December 2026.
  • RumorThe famous alien "races" (Greys, Nordics, Reptilians, Tall Whites, Mantids) come from abductee and contactee stories of the 1950s–90s, mixed with pop culture and some documented US government disinformation. None of them has any physical evidence behind it.
  • RumorThe idea that "Russia or China will disclose first" has no credible source. Their documented programs treat UFOs as an air-defense problem. China's 2022 FAST "alien signal" turned out to be human radio interference. The Micius satellite is a quantum-communication system for human use. Nobody has detected a "quantum alien signal."
  • The honest odds (our subjective consensus) of official confirmation backed by released evidence: US about 1% within 1 year, 2–3% within 5 years, about 4% within 10 years. Add about 1 point to the 5- and 10-year figures if the Review Board becomes law. Russia and China are each under 1%, about 1%, and about 2%.
The one idea to keep in your headReleases and hearings are not disclosure. PURSUE, the National Archives UAP collection, and congressional hearings are transparency processes. Disclosure would mean an official confirmation of NHI backed by evidence that outsiders can check.

2Where US disclosure actually stands

The US is the only country running a public, ongoing UFO (now "UAP," for unidentified anomalous phenomena) process. The machinery has grown a lot since 2017. Its official conclusion has not changed.

PURSUE: the 2026 file releases

  • FactOn President Trump's order, the Department of War (DoW), ODNI, DOE, NASA, and the FBI are releasing UAP files on a rolling basis at war.gov/UFO. Release 1 came out May 8, 2026 and Release 6 on Sep 18, 2026.
  • FactThe site itself calls these "unresolved cases": the government was "unable to make a definitive determination." That means not enough data to explain them, which is different from evidence of aliens.
  • ReportedsecondaryOne UFO-news site reports that AARO withheld in full the written records behind 46 videos a House task force had asked for (FOIA case 26-F-1892, Sep 30, 2026). Critics argue that releasing videos without the paperwork behind them is thin transparency. This hasn't been confirmed in a primary source (Dead of Night Dispatch).

AARO, the Pentagon's UAP office

  • FactFY2025 annual report (posted Jul 20, 2026): 319 new reports, 1,870 total on file, and 370 cases resolved (114 new plus 256 older), all ordinary. 238 were satellites catching the sun ("flaring"). The part headlines skip: 191 of the 319 new reports went into an "active archive" because there wasn't enough data, 9 were referred for more analysis, and a Virginia Navy case (about 100 airborne UAP) is still under active investigation. "All resolved cases were ordinary" is not the same as "nothing is unexplained." (PDF)
  • FactHistorical Record Report Vol. 1 (Mar 2024): no evidence that any US government investigation confirmed extraterrestrial technology. It found reverse-engineering claims unsubstantiated and traced several of them to real classified programs that witnesses misread (report; DoD statement). Vol. 2 is still not on AARO's product list.
  • FactKONA BLUE was a proposed DHS program to handle "non-human biologics." According to AARO it was never approved or funded, and the proposal itself rested on the same unverified "legacy program" claims (AARO UAP Records).

Laws: what passed, what got stripped

  • FY2024 NDAA · Dec 2023FactThe Schumer–Rounds UAP Disclosure Act proposed an independent Review Board plus federal eminent domain over "technologies of unknown origin" and "biological evidence" of NHI. The conference committee stripped both. What survived was a UAP Records Collection at the National Archives (P.L. 118-31 §§1841–1843; S.Amdt.797; NARA).
  • FY2025 NDAAFactSchumer–Rounds was reintroduced and not enacted. details not re-verified
  • FY2026 NDAA · Dec 18, 2025ReportedsecondaryIncludes UAP provisions on NORAD/NORTHCOM intercept briefings going back to 2004, AARO data access, and accounting for classification guides. No Disclosure Act. Sourced only to a tracker (Disclosure Diaries).
  • FY2027 NDAA · Jul 22, 2026FactThe House passed H.R. 8800, 216–212 (CRS IN12704). Reportedsecondary: confirm in official textIt reportedly includes a UAP Disclosure Act version (a Burlison amendment with a Review Board, eminent domain, $20M, and a 2030 sunset). The CRS report does not mention UAP language, so check the Congress.gov engrossed text before relying on this.
  • Senate · Jun–Jul 2026FactThe Schumer/Rounds amendment S.Amdt.6344 (with a Review Board) was filed Jun 24 and never called up (ChamberLight). Cloture on S. 4784 failed 50–46 on Jul 14 (CRS).

Plus: Factthe National Archives UAP collection (Record Group 615) keeps growing through rolling transfers (archives.gov/uap).

Even if they survive, the law creates a records process, not evidence. That's why it moves our odds only about 1 point.

3The "races" lore

If you've spent time on UFO YouTube, you've met the cast. Every type below traces back to a specific book, contactee, or hypnosis session, and you can usually date it. None is supported by physical or biological evidence. They're worth knowing as culture and as a case study in how stories spread.

👽 The Greys

TestimonyLore Claimed home: Zeta Reticuli
Origin
Betty & Barney Hill's abduction story, New Hampshire, Sep 1961. Marjorie Fish later matched Betty's "star map" to Zeta Reticuli. Popularized by The Interrupted Journey (1966), Strieber's Communion (1987), Hopkins, Jacobs, and Mack.
Sources
Hill incident · Grey alien
Skeptic's take: an Outer Limits episode with a similar alien ("The Bellero Shield," 1964) aired 12 days before Barney's hypnosis session. The star-map match is contested and was weakened by newer star data.

✨ The Nordics

TestimonyLore Tall, blond, "space brothers"
Origin
1950s contactees, above all George Adamski's "Orthon" from Venus (1952; Flying Saucers Have Landed, 1953). Later Billy Meier's "Pleiadians" in the 1970s.
Sources
Adamski · Nordic aliens
Skeptic's take: planetary science rules out Venusian civilizations, and Meier's photos were shown to be models.

🦎 The Reptilians

Lore Shape-shifters "among us"
Origin
Pulp fiction (R.E. Howard's serpent men, 1929), scattered abduction reports (e.g., Herbert Schirmer, 1967), and the full conspiracy version in David Icke's The Biggest Secret (1999).
Source
Reptilian conspiracy theory
Skeptic's take: scholars and the ADL point out that Icke's version recycles antisemitic tropes. It's the least "UFO" and most "conspiracy" of the bunch.

🏜️ The Tall Whites

Testimony Supposedly near Nellis AFB, Nevada
Origin
Charles James Hall's Millennial Hospitality series (from 2002). Hall says he met them while working as a USAF weather observer at Indian Springs in the 1960s.
Source
Wikipedia: Tall whites
Skeptic's take: one source, no corroboration.

🦗 The Mantids / Insectoids

TestimonyLore "Supervisors" in abduction accounts

🌀 Hybrids & "Interdimensionals"

Plot twist: some of the myth was government-made
  • FactIn the 1980s, Air Force agent Richard Doty fed researcher Paul Bennewitz fabricated material, including "Aquarius" and MJ-12-style documents (case summary secondary).
  • FactAARO Vol. 1 found a "leaked" 1961 intelligence estimate (SNIE) to be inauthentic (AARO Vol. 1).
  • FactThe Wall Street Journal (Jun 2025) reported doctored saucer photos near Area 51 and "Yankee Blue" hazing briefings that told some officers alien programs were real (WSJ; archive). caveat In a FOIA response, DoD said it found no record of the 2023 "stop" memo the WSJ described (Black Vault). Treat that detail as disputed.

4Russia and China: will they disclose first?

The rumor goes like this: Moscow and Beijing have been quietly studying UFOs, maybe even hunting for "quantum" alien signals, and they'll scoop Washington. Here's what's documented and what isn't.

Documented: the Soviet Union and Russia

  • FactSETKA (about 1978–1990) ran two tracks, one under the Defense Ministry and one under the Academy of Sciences, to study "anomalous atmospheric phenomena." Yuri Platov took part, and his conclusion was that most reports resolved to rocket launches and atmospheric effects (Platov; SETKA summary secondary).
  • FactThe famous 1977 Petrozavodsk "jellyfish" was a Kosmos rocket launch from Plesetsk.
  • Testimony"Thread-3" (1991): the KGB-files story is documented mostly through secondary and archive sources, and no verified primary document has surfaced (archive item; 8NewsNow low quality). Nothing in the Soviet record points to NHI.

Documented: China

  • FactA 2021 South China Morning Post report described a PLA-affiliated study (2019) on using AI to track "unidentified air conditions" for air defense. The researchers themselves said aliens were "unlikely". The concern is foreign drones and spoofing (SCMP; The Debrief not re-verified).
  • FactA civilian China UFO Research Organization (CURO) has existed since 1980. current status unverified

The FAST "alien signal" of 2022

FactResearchers using China's giant FAST radio telescope ran a targeted exoplanet scan (including Kepler-438) and reported narrowband candidate signals in the state-run Science and Technology Daily. The article was then deleted. Co-author Dan Werthimer (UC Berkeley) said the signals were "radio interference… from terrestrials" (Live Science; Futurism; Popular Mechanics; The Conversation). A separate study of FAST's 2019 survey data (arXiv 2305.16356) improved how interference gets filtered out and listed candidates that still need checking. None has been confirmed. (Background on FAST interference filtering: sciopen.)

Micius and "quantum communication signals"

  • FactMicius (launched 2016) is a real quantum-key-distribution satellite. In 2017 it sent entangled photons to ground stations 1,203 km apart (Science, 2017; news). It's engineering for human secure communication, not a SETI instrument.
  • FactQuantum SETI exists as theory. Physicists Berera and Calderón-Figueroa showed that quantum coherence in photons could survive interstellar distances, with X-rays the best band. They also stress that detecting and interpreting such a signal would take "a fair amount of guesswork" (PRD 2020; PRD 2022; arXiv).
  • RumorClaims linking Micius to alien communication have no credible source. No paper reports a quantum-coherent signal of extraterrestrial origin. The closest "signal" stories, FAST 2022 and Parkes "BLC1," used ordinary (classical) radio, and both were ruled interference.
How the hype gets built"Quantum" + "signal" + "China" mashes together three real things: Micius, a theory paper, and the FAST blip. None of the three supports the combined story.

Why "they'll disclose first" is weak

  • RumorThe claim recurs on social media and podcasts. No official signal and no credible source was found.
  • The incentives point the other way. An authoritarian government gains nothing by admitting it doesn't control its own airspace. A one-sided announcement without shared samples would be dismissed abroad as propaganda. Both governments get more value from framing US UFO talk as cover for American weapons, or as "ghost" spoofing.
  • The Western reading would be suspicion, not awe. If Moscow or Beijing announced aliens, the rational US and allied response would be to treat it as an information operation or as cover for advanced technology, unless they shared samples for independent lab testing.
  • According to AARO, foreign official efforts have reached the same no-NHI conclusions.

5The skeptic's case

The strongest skeptical argument isn't "people are dumb." It has three legs: the famous videos have ordinary explanations, key witnesses are secondhand or contested, and part of the mythology was deliberately planted (see the box in section 3).

The whistleblower ledger

  • Testimony · secondhandDavid Grusch. Under oath he said his claims about an NHI-craft program came from other people, and that he could name witnesses and locations only in a classified setting (transcript; opening statement). In 2022 the Intelligence Community Inspector General called his complaint "credible and urgent." That phrase is a legal threshold for forwarding a complaint to Congress, not a finding that the claims are true. no primary link in our sources AARO Vol. 1 found the specific claims it received inaccurate. Corroborated: his employment history and that he filed the complaint. Fair caveat: AARO can't disprove claims it was never given, and his supporters question AARO's independence.
  • Testimony · role disputedLuis Elizondo. DoD spokesperson Susan Gough said he had "no assigned responsibilities" for AATIP and that the DIA ran it (Black Vault). Elizondo disputes this, and his FOIA-released emails asking for a correction exist (DoD FOIA emails). Both sides have documents, so his role is disputed, not settled. His claims about retrieved craft are uncorroborated.
  • Fact (event)David Fravor / Tic Tac. The Navy authenticated the videos in 2020 (AARO imagery). What the object was remains unresolved (see table).
  • TestimonyRyan Graves. Reporting confirms the 2014–15 East Coast sightings happened. It doesn't establish what they were, and the related GIMBAL/GOFAST videos have strong ordinary explanations.
  • TestimonyTim Gallaudet: recalls a "Go Fast" email, and no document has been released. Karl Nell: publicly backs Grusch, with no documents. Rep. Burlison and others say they were briefed. That means they heard claims, not that they saw evidence.
  • Rumor · unverifiedJay Stratton (Oct 2026). Secondary reports say the former UAP Task Force director claimed on Fox to have seen NHI craft and beings (ufouap.com secondary). We couldn't find the primary interview. AARO's former and current directors (Kirkpatrick, Phillips) say no legacy program exists. This becomes Testimony only if the video or transcript turns up.
  • Testimony · discreditedBob Lazar: no MIT or Caltech records support his claimed degrees (MIT check; "Lazar Flaws"). Philip Corso (The Day After Roswell) and Steven Greer are criticized for factual errors and uncorroborated claims (Skeptical Inquirer).

6The strongest case for "something there"

Fairness check. Here's the best honest version of the believer's case.

  • There's a real unexplained remainder. AARO and ODNI still list cases they couldn't resolve (191 went into AARO's archive this year alone), and the Virginia maritime case is open. AARO says that's a data problem, but "not enough data" cuts both ways (ODNI 2021 URL unverified).
  • Nimitz is genuinely unresolved. Multiple trained aviators gave consistent accounts, and the raw radar data has never been released.
  • Credentialed people testified under oath, and Congress responded. A bipartisan Senate leadership pair wrote eminent-domain language for "technologies of unknown origin." Supporters argue the stripping of that language in 2023 is itself suspicious (Schumer–Rounds colloquy; Covington analysis). Counter: it reflects ordinary turf and classification politics, and it produced no evidence.
  • The government has lied about this topic before. Documented myth-seeding means official denials deserve skepticism too, including AARO's.
  • NASA and AARO both want better sensors and less stigma (NASA study), which is an admission that the data is poor.
Honest bottom line from both sidesThe record says "no evidence found," not "proven impossible."

Why the numbers look like this

  • Base rate: zero official confirmations in 75+ years, including from investigations with full access.
  • The trend in the data: every case AARO resolves turns out ordinary, and more data means more resolutions.
  • Why the US numbers were cut: the original US ~5% at 5 years ran 2.5× above its own base-rate reasoning. Its only upward driver, the Review Board, hasn't been enacted. PURSUE raises the odds of more files, not of confirmation.
  • Russia and China: their incentives favor treating UAP as adversary drones, and they have no public process at all. A disclosure from them would also face a credibility problem abroad.
  • Other governments (e.g., France, the UK, Brazil, Japan): none has published anything beyond ordinary findings, and AARO says foreign efforts reached the same conclusions. That's why these sit at or just below the US.
  • The quiet assumption: if NHI isn't real, these numbers stay low no matter how much gets released.
